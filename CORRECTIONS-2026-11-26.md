# Corrections to Geometric Politics, 2026-11-26

These corrections were made on 2026-11-26 at the author's direction after a review of the series.

Paths are under `manuscript/`. District 7 is a fictional district (front matter, "A Note on District 7"), and Appendix B.6 generates its electorate as a synthetic sample of 2,000 voters.

## Reconciled

1. **Campaign Gradient Theorem** (`part_ii/ch06_campaigns_heuristic.md`, Theorem 2 and its proof). The text gave two forms and called them equivalent. The first minimizes the mean squared projected distance between electorate and candidate. The second maximized the candidate's squared offset from the mean divided by the variance, which selects the axis where the candidate is farthest from the mean and is not the same criterion. With variances 4 and 1 and the candidate at (0, 2), the first form chooses axis 1 (4 against 5) and the second chooses axis 2 (0 against 4). The second form is now the direct expansion of the first, the minimum of the variance along the axis plus the squared offset of the candidate from the mean along the axis. The expansion in the proof had a wrong cross term and now reads as the identity E|<v - x_C, e>|^2 = e^T Sigma e + <x_C - mu, e>^2, checked by algebra. "A generalized eigenvalue problem" now names the matrix, Sigma + (x_C - mu)(x_C - mu)^T. The prose of the theorem and its three predictions already described the minimizing form and are unchanged.
2. **Three winners, not two or five** (`part_ii/ch05_voting_contraction.md`, "District 7: Five Voting Systems, Five Outcomes" and chapter summary; `part_v/ch17_feedback_general_theory.md`, "District 7: Four Lessons"). The text said the five systems produce "five different winners", then "two different winners (A, B, or C)", and Chapter 17 repeated "five different winners". The five results listed are B, A, A, C, C. All three places now say three different winners.
3. **Ranked-choice count in Chapter 5** (`part_ii/ch05_voting_contraction.md`, item 3 of the same section). The text had C eliminated at 28% and A winning with 52%. Chapter 13 prints the full transfer table for the same election, and it adds up: C stands at 29% when eliminated and A wins 54 to 46. Chapter 5 now gives 29% and 54%.
4. **Plurality winner in Chapter 13** (`part_iv/ch13_ranked_choice.md`, running example and "District 7: The First RCV Election"). The text said Candidate A would have won under plurality with 28%. The first-choice shares in Chapter 5, and in Chapter 13's own rounds, are A 28, B 31, C 18, D 14, E 9, so B leads. The same paragraph already says A "was not the most popular first choice". Both passages now say B would have won under plurality with 31%, ahead of A at 28%.
5. **Chapter 13 summary** (`part_iv/ch13_ranked_choice.md`, chapter summary). The summary said the moderate candidate won District 7's first ranked-choice election. In the chapter A wins that election and the moderate C wins only in the variation. The summary now says so.

The text described the result of Geometric Communication as a displacement of judgment by 8.9 standard deviations, "the largest framing effect ever measured in the moral reasoning literature", and set the displacement factor delta to about 8.9. Section 14.2.2 of Geometric Communication reports a Fisher-combined significance of 8.9 sigma across five models and two framing types, with a displacement of 15 to 25 percent of the scale range. The four sentences now say that, and the sentence equating delta with 8.9 says that 8.9 sigma is a significance level and not a value of delta.

## Unresolved

Nothing below was changed, because the right value could not be established from the repository.

1. **Information preserved by ranked-choice voting.** Theorem 7 (Chapter 13) and Appendix C.3 give min(d, k-1)/d, which is 4/6 or 67 percent for the five-candidate District 7 election. Chapter 5 ("Information Preservation Ranking") and the table of Chapter 15 print 45 percent. Raising it to 67 would put ranked choice above Condorcet (50) and multi-member proportional (60) and break the ordering both chapters rely on. The author needs to say how the percentages of the two lists are computed.
2. **Political Bond Index of plurality.** The comparison table of Chapter 15 gives plurality 2.8 and ranked choice 1.4. The scenarios of the same chapter give plurality on Map A 1.2 and ranked choice on Map A 0.9, and the subgroup values (1.8, 0.6, 1.5) and Chapter 16 (1.2) agree with the scenarios. The table would agree if it were computed on Map B, where plurality is 2.8, but the text does not say so.
3. **Eastfield under Map B.** Chapter 15 gives Eastfield's voters 3.5 "compared to 2.4 for the district as a whole", an excess of 1.1. Map B is 2.8 elsewhere in the chapter. Under Map B Eastfield is moved to another district, so 2.4 may refer to that district. The author needs to say which district is meant.
4. **District 7 covariance matrix, eigenvalues and code.** Chapter 4 prints a covariance matrix and the eigenvalues 3.2, 2.8, 1.9, 1.5, 1.0, 0.6 (29, 25, 17, 14, 9, 5 percent). The eigenvalues of the printed matrix are 3.41, 3.26, 1.78, 1.26, 0.84, 0.45 (31, 30, 16, 11, 8, 4 percent). The synthetic electorate of Appendix B.6, run as printed, gives a different covariance matrix again, with shares 50, 23, 11, 6, 5, 5 percent. Under each version four eigenvalues reach 85 percent. "No single dimension explains even 30%" holds only for the printed eigenvalues. Appendix B says all computational results in the main text can be verified with its code, and that is not so for this matrix. The author needs to choose the generating model and regenerate the matrix, the eigenvalues, and the sentences built on them (Chapters 4, 6 and 15).
5. **Distances in the District 7 examples.** With the printed centroids and the printed covariance matrix, the Mahalanobis distance between the party centroids of Chapter 8 is 2.44, not 1.5. The perceived distance comes out near 4.3, close to the printed 4.2. The same sentence lists the largest separations as d2, d5 and d3 and omits d1, whose separation is 2.2, the largest, while the chapter's narrative says the district is united on d1. The Route 9 to exurban distance of Chapter 9 is 2.71 (printed 4.2), and the bloc-to-candidate table of Chapter 7 does not match either the Mahalanobis or the Euclidean distances. "1.5 standard deviations" recurs through Chapter 8. These depend on item 4.
6. **Proof of the Polarization Illusion Theorem.** The construction in the second paragraph of the proof does not work as written. The density given is the product of a function of the first coordinate and a Gaussian in the others, so every one-dimensional projection is that function convolved with a Gaussian, and it is unimodal whenever the density is. The third paragraph is not a complete argument. The statement of the theorem is plausible (a horseshoe-shaped high-density region projects to two peaks), but a correct construction is new mathematics and is left to the author.
7. **Chapter 13 variation.** In the variation where C wins, A is eliminated at 35% as "now lowest" and its transfers (22 to C, 13 to B) give C 57 and B 43. That puts C at 35 and B at 30 before the transfer, so B, not A, is lowest. The shares need to be rewritten.
8. **DW-NOMINATE and 93 percent.** Chapter 1 says the first dimension explains about 93 percent of roll-call variance. Chapter 4 says two dimensions explain about 93 percent, and then compares 93 percent with a first eigenvalue. A source is needed.
9. **Gerrymandering Surgery Theorem.** Theorem 5 calls a map a gerrymander whenever its index exceeds the ensemble expectation, which about half of all random maps do, and by which Map C (2.0 against 1.9) is a gerrymander. The procedure of the same chapter and of Appendix C uses the 95th percentile. The theorem also writes GI(D_neutral) for a quantity it defines as an expected index. The author needs to choose the criterion.
10. **Appendix B.5.** The code listing implements the former second form of the Campaign Gradient Theorem (the maximum of the ratio). Code listings were not edited. The listing and its docstring now disagree with Theorem 2 and need to be rewritten to minimize e^T Sigma e + <x_C - mu, e>^2.
11. **Series counts.** The front matter says "Book 10", lists seven titles as "Books 4 through 9", and speaks of "a ten-volume series" and "twelve books". Chapter 18 says "Twelve books in total", names eleven, and says "ten domains" where it names nine. The README lists thirteen volumes and Politics is not the last. That is the Scalar Irrecoverability Theorem. The No Escape Theorem of Geometric Ethics concerns a structurally contained agent. The section needs the author's restatement.
